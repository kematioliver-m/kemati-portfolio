Instructional Framework

The 5E Model

Every lesson I design follows the 5E instructional model — a research-based framework that structures learning as a progressive cycle. Each stage builds on the last, taking students from initial curiosity to confident application.

E
Engage

Capture student interest and surface prior knowledge. Connect the lesson to real-world scenarios — healthcare databases, retail data, workplace tools.

In practice: Kahoot quizzes, essential questions, real-life scenarios
E
Explore

Students get hands-on with the concept. I step back and let them experiment, make mistakes, and discover how things work for themselves.

In practice: Lab activities, guided discovery, peer collaboration
E
Explain

Guided questions help students connect their exploration to the formal concept. I clarify, address misconceptions, and build shared understanding.

In practice: Guiding questions, demonstrations, class discussion
E
Elaborate

Students apply the concept to new, more complex situations — extending their understanding and developing independence and creative thinking.

In practice: Scenarios, extended tasks, breakout rooms
E
Evaluate

Both teacher and student assess whether learning objectives were achieved. Reflection is built into every session — for students and for me.

In practice: Checklists, quizzes, teacher reflection, exit activities

Values

Core Beliefs

Student‑Centred Learning

Students are not passive recipients of information. I design lessons that invite participation, encourage questions, and give learners agency over their own understanding. I call students by name, acknowledge their contributions, and create space for them to direct their own learning.

Technology as a Tool

Technology should enhance learning, not replace it. I use digital tools — Kahoot, breakout rooms, slide presentations, live demonstrations — to create interactive, multi-modal experiences. My ICT background allows me to connect classroom content directly to real workplace applications.

Inclusion & Differentiation

Every classroom has visual, auditory, and kinaesthetic learners. I plan for this deliberately — demonstrations for visual learners, verbal explanations for auditory learners, and hands-on lab activities for kinaesthetic learners. Individual check-ins ensure no student is left behind.

In the Classroom

Philosophy Into Practice

"The feedback I received was constructive and affirmed that what I have learned in theory is, in fact, what needs to be translated into practice. The experience has made me a better educator."

— From TP1 Section C Reflection, 2025

01
Real-World Scenarios

Healthcare databases in Microsoft Access, retail data in Excel, workplace documents in Word — every topic is anchored to a context students recognise and will encounter professionally.

02
Individual Check-Ins

During every lab session, I move around the room (or the breakout rooms) to check each student's progress personally — addressing confusion before it becomes a barrier.

03
Respectful, Professional Tone

I call students by name, acknowledge their experiences, and maintain a calm and encouraging classroom climate. Students are more willing to attempt difficult tasks when they feel safe to make mistakes.

04
Reflective Practice

I evaluate every lesson I teach — identifying what worked, what didn't, and what to adjust next time. Growth as a teacher requires the same honest self-assessment I ask of my students.
